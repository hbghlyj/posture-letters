# Historical 24-pose construction map

Source: *The Comical Hotch-Potch, or the Alphabet turn’d Posture-Master* (1782). The print has 24 cells; J and U are absent.

This map records the visible construction of each figure. Historical fidelity takes priority over modern anatomical plausibility.

| Glyph | Construction visible in the print |
|---|---|
| A | Editorial yoga-alphabet A. A standing wide-legged forward fold seen from the side: the lifted hips are the apex, the two spread legs are the diagonals, and the torso and reaching arms fold down the left leg as the crossbar, with the head hanging low beside the hands. |
| B | Editorial yoga-alphabet B. Seen from behind: the standing leg and torso are the stem, the hand-on-hip elbow is the upper bowl, and the bent knee is the lower bowl. Replaces the print's crouched two-arm clasp. |
| C | Editorial yoga-alphabet C. A kneeling backbend: shins on the floor, thighs the left stem, arched torso and thrown-back head opening to the right, arms wrapped at the waist. Replaces the print's kneeling C-arch with arms over the top. |
| D | Editorial Ustrasana (camel pose), replacing the print's impossible ring backbend. Kneeling on both shins; thighs form the left stem; the spine arches back so the head is thrown back as the top of the bowl; both hands reach down and grasp the heels. |
| E | Kneeling in profile facing right, shins and tops of the feet flat along the floor as the bottom bar and the spine upright as the stem. The head is tucked down and forward, sitting low. Both arms are folded at the elbow: the upper arm rises and its forearm lies horizontally just above the tucked head as the top bar, while the other forearm projects forward at mid-chest height as the shorter middle prong. The tucked head fills the stem-to-top-bar junction. |
| F | Editorial yoga-alphabet F (replaces the 1782 standing figure). Balancing on one straight leg as the stem, both arms extended forward at the top as the upper bar, and the folded lifted leg — thigh forward, shin tucked down — as the middle bar. |
| G | Editorial yoga-alphabet G. Seated in side view with the spine curled into a deep rounded C making the left bowl, the knees drawn up close to close the lower right, and both arms arcing overhead to the right with the hands dropping into a short downward hook as the spur of the G. |
| H | Editorial yoga-alphabet H (replaces the 1782 pair of figures). The horizontal torso is the crossbar; the raised leg above and the kneeling shin below make the left stem; the raised arm above and the planted arm below make the right stem. |
| I | Standing upright with the legs pressed together as the stem, both arms raised and crossed above the head so the forearms and open hands make the top serif, and the feet turned outward to make the bottom serif. |
| J | Editorial yoga-alphabet J (the 1782 print has no J). A curled side-profile pose: both straight joined legs rise as the single tall right stem, flexed feet point left as a short top serif, and the hips, back, and merged arms sweep through the broad rounded bottom hook. The naturally upright profile head and smooth neck form its compact lower-left terminal without closing the counter. |
| K | Editorial yoga-alphabet K. Seated in side view with the spine held straight and vertical as the stem, one leg extended straight up and out to the top right and the other straight down and out to the bottom right, the two legs springing from the hips as the arms of the K. |
| L | Kneeling side profile. The head, straight neck and vertical torso form the tall pillar. At the base of the torso the posture hinges sharply: the knees turn directly forward and bend completely, dropping the thighs vertically and pivoting the lower body into the horizontal plane, so the shins and ankles stretch out along the floor as the bottom bar. The feet act as a serif at the end of the stroke, heels and toes giving a slight vertical terminal. |
| M | Two legs make the whole letter, following the 1782 print. A figure folded completely double and seen from behind: the two raised knees are the peaks, the calves drop to flat feet as the outer stems, and the thighs slope down and inward to the hanging hips to make the central V. |
| N | Editorial yoga-alphabet N from the 2 Way Stretch poster. In side profile, one straight supporting arm descends from the shoulder to a planted hand as the left stem, the naturally aligned head and torso descend diagonally into the low hips, and one joined raised leg rises as the tall right stem. The planted hand and rounded hip share a baseline, and anatomical chest, waist, pelvis, thigh, and calf transitions keep the diagonal organic rather than ruled. |
| O | Editorial yoga-alphabet O. A full bow: sit on the floor, both legs bent up, both hands clasping the toes, closing a ring with a large natural counter. Replaces the print's standing backbend ring. |
| P | Editorial yoga-alphabet P. A standing backbend: planted legs are the stem; the arched torso, hanging head and clasped hands close the bowl against the knees. Replaces the print's I-stem plus one looping arm. |
| Q | Editorial bow-as-Q (yoga-alphabet / full bow with one supporting arm). The prone backbend, raised feet and grasping arm close the ring; the other arm plants on the floor as the lower-right tail. Compact head with bun looks up beside the reaching arm. Replaces the print's inverted ring. |
| R | Editorial yoga-alphabet R. The same standing backbend as P, with the front leg stepped forward as the diagonal. Replaces the print's I-stem plus looping arm and kicked leg. |
| S | Kneeling backbend in right-facing side profile. Both knees meet at the lower right while the closely overlapped shins and pointed feet rest on the ground and extend left as the lower terminal. The thighs rise into the pelvis and the torso curves deeply left and upward through the middle of the letter. The profile head sits inside the upper-left opening, and two closely overlapped arms sweep overhead before the hands descend at the upper right to complete the upper hook. |
| T | Editorial yoga-alphabet T (replaces the 1782 standing figure). Standing upright seen from behind: legs together as the vertical stem and both arms stretched straight out level with the shoulders as the horizontal bar. |
| U | Editorial yoga-alphabet U, from `reference-crops/two-way-stretch/image_6a52f7c6.png` (the 1782 print has no U). A side-profile gymnast lying on the back: both arms rise as the left stem and finish in a compact inward hand serif, the rounded hips and torso make the bowl, and both joined legs rise as the right stem to pointed feet. The naturally aligned profile head sits on the left stem without closing the open counter; both stems reach the same height. |
| V | Editorial yoga-alphabet V from `reference-crops/two-way-stretch/V.png`. Two equal legs rise from a compact rounded buttocks apex as the strokes of a capital V, with natural thigh-to-calf taper and outward-turned shod feet as the upper terminals. This follows the 1782 inverted-leg topology more closely than the previous side-profile V-sit, but uses the buttocks rather than the head as the low vertex. |
| W | Reference-conditioned reconstruction of the 1782 W topology. Seen from the front while hinging the torso forward toward the camera: the compact head and shoulders make the high centre peak; each arm overlaps one long thigh as an inner diagonal; each hand conceals a grounded knee at a low vertex; and each naturally shorter calf rises alone as an outer diagonal, visibly slimmer than the muscular thigh before tapering to the ankle. Each anterior ankle faces forward while its pointed foot turns modestly outward, showing the dorsolateral top; the historic print's overlong outer bars and knee inclusion are not copied. The direct silhouette conversion retains the photographed calf–thigh overlap without carving the junction. |
| X | Editorial yoga-alphabet X (traced, not from the 1782 print). A wide forward fold seen from behind: the legs spread into an inverted V with both feet flat on the floor, the torso folded down so the head hangs at the centre, and both arms stretched up and out to the upper corners. |
| Y | Editorial yoga-alphabet Y (replaces the 1782 standing figure with raised arms). A headstand with the torso as the stem and the two legs split wide into a V as the arms of the letter. |
| Z | Editorial yoga-alphabet Z (replaces the 1782 seated figure). Kneeling with both arms reaching horizontally at head height as the top bar, the leaning torso and thighs as the diagonal, and the shins and feet flat along the floor as the bottom bar. |

## Photographic body references

Several glyphs were additionally checked against modern photographs of the same
posture, used only to correct proportion and joint anatomy — never to override
the print's pose topology:

- A spread-eagle silhouette informed **X** and **Y**: one shared limb width for
  arms and legs, both under torso width, and open hands at the raised terminals.
- A handshake photograph of two people standing in profile informed **H**:
  standing torso-to-leg balance, vertical upper arms, and a forearm-only
  crossbar at elbow height.
- A standing frontal figure informed **T**, and the same balance was then
  applied to the other upright standing poses **F** and **P**: hips near the
  middle of the stem with legs matching the trunk in length.
- A reference-conditioned side-profile gymnast now supplies **S** as one
  coherent photographed body: grounded shins and feet form the lower terminal,
  the backbend carries the middle curve, and the overlapping arms make the
  upper hook. The silhouette was converted directly from that corrected pose.

## Current font-outline refinement note

All visible legs now share the print’s period costume anatomy without sharing generic geometry: full short trousers gather into a clear cuff just below each source-positioned knee, stockinged lower legs widen into a noticeable calf muscle, an inset engraved contour reinforces the gastrocnemius/shin transition, and ankles taper into the historically directed shoes. The system follows each pose through standing, seated, kneeling, horizontal, and inverted orientations rather than straightening the body into standardized strokes. M retains custom exaggerated calf pillars with explicit knee cuffs; N now follows the 2 Way Stretch balance as a single clean exterior whose planted arm, organic torso diagonal, and raised leg make the three strokes; and Q’s circular lower-body construction now consists of two cuffed, bent legs with tapered calves and joined apex shoes. O alone preserves the source’s hidden aerial lower torso with only compressed feet visible.

The current font follows A’s continuous forward fold while reversing both shoes left and differentiating wide breeches, knee cuffs, calves, and shin cuts. B now separates the planted leg from the outward-and-inward bent right leg, keeps a visible knee, and shortens both loop arms and the lower-bowl leg to near-normal anatomy. D and H mirror only their asymmetric facial and hat details, preserving their established body topology. E extends its vertical torso directly into the baseline leg. I keeps its rigid source stance while restoring realistic head, trunk, arm, thigh, and calf ratios. K drops its upper elbow before a rising forearm whose compact palm projects only the thumb and index as sharp serifs, with the other three fingers folded inward; full short breeches and engraved muscle contours remain on both lower legs. M keeps its face, cupping hands, paired buttocks, central cleft, cuffed muscular pillars, and removes the former hanging center appendage. N puts its planted supporting hand and rounded low hip on one baseline, keeps the profile head naturally aligned over the shoulder, and carries the diagonal through visible chest, waist, abdomen, and pelvis curves rather than a ruled stroke. O hides the lower torso, inverts its facial hierarchy, lifts its pupils toward the counter, smooths the shoulders and elbows into the ring, marks visible side knee/elbow joints, and gives the apex a visible interlocking hand seam. P shortens both separately tapered backward arm routes while retaining elbow, sleeve, palm, and clasp details above its cuffed legs and outward shoes. Q rebuilds its ring as articulated inverted legs, inverts its bottom face like O, keeps the tail arm at a normal anatomical length, and gives that tail a visible hand. R turns its planted shoe clearly to the source-right orientation. S now follows one directly converted side-profile gymnast, with the grounded shins and feet sweeping left, the thighs and backbend carrying the lower-right and middle curves, and the overlapped arms forming the upper-right hook. W now follows a photographed forward-hinged reconstruction of the historic topology: one broad arm-over-thigh inner stroke and one shorter, visibly slimmer calf-only outer stroke meet naturally at each hand-concealed grounded knee without a carved junction; each anterior ankle faces forward while its pointed foot turns modestly outward to show the dorsolateral top. X shortens its raised arms to a normal diagonal reach while keeping its long spread legs, and Y shortens both the raised arms and torso while lengthening the close paired leg stem. G/O/Q remain free of the former dangling decorative artifacts; O’s apex seam and Q’s cuff/calf cuts are functional anatomy. H also keeps separate upper-arm and thigh roots, with open side gaps for contour clarity.

Across A–Z, hats and shoes now act as anatomical serif terminals: cocked-hat brims project from source-visible hats, and shoes follow the toe directions visible in the source at the actual feet. They remain attached to their body endpoints instead of moving to conventional type-stroke corners. F, K, and R point both profile shoes right; E, G, and L turn their shoes upward; P and X use outward left/right pairs. O uses compressed outward shoes for its aerial foreshortening, while Q’s compact pair points inward at the ring apex. N uses the planted supporting hand as its lower-left terminal while the flexed foot terminates the raised right leg. M keeps its small hatless upward face in the central dip and uses outward shoes on its outer calf pillars rather than inventing a displaced hat. These are standard scalable outlines with punched contour details, not separate raster artwork.

## Editorial letters

- **J** and **U** do not occur in the print. **U** is now a yoga-alphabet cobra with lifted shins rather than a 1782 source pose.
- **A** replaces the print's two-person Mitelli pose with a vertically flipped Paripurna Navasana (full boat). It is an editorial interpolation, not a 1782 source pose.
- **C** replaces the print's kneeling C-arch with a yoga-alphabet kneeling backbend. It is an editorial interpolation, not a 1782 source pose.
- **D** replaces the print's impossible ring backbend with Ustrasana (camel pose). It is an editorial interpolation, not a 1782 source pose.
- **N** replaces the print's piked headstand with the one-person side-profile balance from the 2 Way Stretch yoga alphabet. It is an editorial interpolation, not a 1782 source pose.
- **P** and **R** replace the print's I-stem-plus-loop with a standing backbend (R steps the front leg out as the diagonal). They are editorial interpolations, not 1782 source poses.
- **Q** replaces the print's inverted ring with a prone bow whose supporting arm is the tail. It is an editorial interpolation, not a 1782 source pose.
- If retained for modern A–Z coverage, they must be labeled as editorial interpolations rather than 1782 source poses.
