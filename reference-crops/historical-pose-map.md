# Historical 24-pose construction map

Source: *The Comical Hotch-Potch, or the Alphabet turn’d Posture-Master* (1782). The print has 24 cells; J and U are absent.

This map records the visible construction of each figure. Historical fidelity takes priority over modern anatomical plausibility.

| Glyph | Construction visible in the print |
|---|---|
| A | A complete forward fold. Hips/lower back make the apex. Two close breeched thighs descend on the right, narrow through knee cuffs into stockinged calves, and terminate in two left/inward-pointing shoes. The attached spine and upper torso descend on the left through a continuous neck into the inverted, downward-facing head; its eye/nose turn right and its cocked hat hangs naturally beneath the head. Two separate horizontal arms reach from the shoulders to grip the knees, forming the crossbar. |
| B | Upright figure at the left. The head sits at the upper-left and both arms curl around to make the small upper bowl. Below the pelvis the planted left leg stays separate; the right thigh bends far outward, rounds the knee, and curves inward again toward its separate foot to make the larger lower bowl. |
| C | Kneeling backward arch. Shins and feet run right along the baseline; thighs/hips and spine sweep up the left curve. The face is inside the upper-left opening, and an arm extends over the top toward the upper-right. |
| D | Fully inverted circular fold. A trousered leg runs across the top and a stockinged calf descends on the left. The torso curves down the right and along the bottom. The head lies outside/under the lower curve with its asymmetric face/hat details directed as in the print, while a bent arm reaches up the left to the foot. |
| E | Seated upright at the left: head and torso form the stem. One arm extends straight right at the top and the second reaches right at mid-height. The broad lower leg lies horizontally along the baseline; the slimmer near leg remains separately visible just above it, with both profile shoes turning upward. |
| F | Standing upright at the left. One arm extends straight right from the shoulder as the top bar, the other reaches right at mid-height, and two close legs form the lower stem. |
| G | Deeper version of C. The body sweeps around the left; the face looks into the counter. Arms meet toward the upper-right, and paired lower legs/feet run right at the bottom, producing the inward G terminals. |
| H | Back-bend/tabletop pose. The horizontal torso and centered upside-down head make the crossbar, with the head/hat asymmetry facing the source direction. Both arms rise vertically at the outer sides; both legs descend vertically to the ground. |
| I | Neutral upright person: head above a rigid straight torso, arms hanging at the sides, and two close straight legs below. The ordinary human head/trunk/leg balance is essential to this deliberately simple posture. |
| K | Upright profile at the left. Torso and near leg create the stem. The upper arm drops to a distinct elbow, then the forearm curves upward into an open upper-right hand: thumb and index project from the palm while the other fingers curl inward. The second arm stays near the body. The far leg steps diagonally lower-right, and both lower legs retain visible calf/shin modeling. |
| L | Seated profile leaning back behind the pelvis. Paired straight legs extend horizontally to the right along the ground, with both shoes turning upward. Hands rest near the lap/knees. |
| M | Intentionally impossible split body. A distinct frontal face looks directly upward at the central dip. Immediately beneath it, two hands flank and hold the separately rounded buttocks around a clean central cleft, with no additional hanging center appendage. Mirrored massive thighs rise toward the two shoulders of the M, then resolve into muscular outer stockinged calf pillars and outward-facing feet at the baseline. |
| N | Inverted curled pose. The head and broad bent knee share the lower ground plane. A folded yellow torso rises at the left; from its high inner corner a sleeved arm and hand descend clearly across the foreground of the long blue diagonal body. The far stockinged calf then rises vertically to its high shoe. |
| O | Inverted ring. The upside-down head and shoulders lie at the bottom center, with the face directed toward the ring interior. Mirrored shoulder, elbow, and arm arcs rise smoothly on both sides, and the hands clasp at the top. Two tiny foreshortened feet remain visible just above the bottom base. |
| P | Standing profile at the left. Head/torso/legs make the stem; two separately visible, normally proportioned arms bend backward through distinct upper and lower elbows and clasp at the right to form the compact upper bowl. The paired shoes turn outward at the base. |
| Q | Inverted lower body. The head/shoulders lie at the bottom; paired short-breeched thighs rise around the ring, bend at explicit side knees, and taper through stockinged calves to small inward shoes joined at the apex. One shoulder-attached arm and hand projects below the lower-right as the Q tail. |
| R | Standing profile at the left. Arms clasp into the upper bowl as in P. One leg remains under the body with its profile shoe pointing right as in the crop, while the other steps diagonally lower-right to make the R leg. |
| S | Kneeling profile. Close shins and feet extend left along the ground; thighs/hips and torso curve up through the middle. The face looks into the upper counter, and one long arm arches overhead toward the upper-right. |
| T | Upright figure. Both arms stretch widely and horizontally to form the crossbar. The torso is central and both close legs descend straight down; they do not spread. |
| V | Upside-down figure. The head is at the bottom apex. Two straight legs spread upward to the outer top corners; the arms lie along/grip the legs near the lower V. |
| W | Upright centered head. The torso splits into the two inner descending strokes; arms reach all the way to two shared-baseline inner hand/contact points, while two long legs rise vertically at the outer sides, creating the four-stroke W. |
| X | Head and short torso occupy the center. Both arms rise diagonally to the upper corners and both legs spread diagonally to the lower corners. |
| Y | Upright head/torso at center. Both arms rise diagonally to form the fork; two close straight legs descend together as the stem. |
| Z | Kneeling diagonal pose. A horizontal arm extends left from the head/shoulder at the top, the torso descends diagonally toward the lower-left, and close bent legs extend right along the ground. |

## Current font-outline refinement note

All visible legs now share the print’s period costume anatomy without sharing generic geometry: full short trousers gather into a clear cuff just below each source-positioned knee, stockinged lower legs widen into a noticeable calf muscle, an inset engraved contour reinforces the gastrocnemius/shin transition, and ankles taper into the historically directed shoes. The system follows each pose through standing, seated, kneeling, horizontal, and inverted orientations rather than straightening the body into standardized strokes. M retains custom exaggerated calf pillars with explicit knee cuffs; N’s blue diagonal reaches a grounded bent knee before turning into the raised gray calf; and Q’s circular lower-body construction now consists of two cuffed, bent legs with tapered calves and joined apex shoes. O alone preserves the source’s hidden aerial lower torso with only compressed feet visible.

The current font follows A’s continuous forward fold while reversing both shoes left and differentiating wide breeches, knee cuffs, calves, and shin cuts. B now separates the planted leg from the outward-and-inward bent right leg that draws its lower bowl. D and H mirror only their asymmetric facial and hat details, preserving their established body topology. E extends its vertical torso directly into the baseline leg. I keeps its rigid source stance while restoring realistic head, trunk, arm, thigh, and calf ratios. K drops its upper elbow before a rising forearm whose compact palm projects only the thumb and index as sharp serifs, with the other three fingers folded inward; full short breeches and engraved muscle contours remain on both lower legs. M keeps its face, cupping hands, paired buttocks, central cleft, cuffed muscular pillars, and removes the former hanging center appendage. N puts head and knee on one ground plane, preserves the raised inner corner, and outlines a human-length foreground sleeve across the blue diagonal with a projecting hand. O hides the lower torso, inverts its facial hierarchy, lifts its pupils toward the counter, smooths the shoulders and elbows into the ring, and gives the apex a visible interlocking hand seam. P shortens both separately tapered backward arm routes while retaining elbow, sleeve, palm, and clasp details above its cuffed legs and outward shoes. Q rebuilds its ring as articulated inverted legs and joins the descending tail arm directly to its shoulder socket. R turns its planted shoe clearly to the source-right orientation. W lowers both inner contacts exactly to the baseline. G/O/Q remain free of the former dangling decorative artifacts; O’s apex seam and Q’s cuff/calf cuts are functional anatomy. H also keeps separate upper-arm and thigh roots, with open side gaps for contour clarity.

Across A–Z, hats and shoes now act as anatomical serif terminals: cocked-hat brims project from source-visible hats, and shoes follow the toe directions visible in the source at the actual feet. They remain attached to their body endpoints instead of moving to conventional type-stroke corners. F, K, and R point both profile shoes right; E, G, and L turn their shoes upward; P and X use outward left/right pairs. O uses compressed outward shoes for its aerial foreshortening, while Q’s compact pair points inward at the ring apex. N’s left shoe stays on the inverted body endpoint while its high right shoe terminates the raised calf. M keeps its small hatless upward face in the central dip and uses outward shoes on its outer calf pillars rather than inventing a displaced hat. These are standard scalable outlines with punched contour details, not separate raster artwork.

## Editorial letters

- **J** and **U** do not occur in the print and therefore cannot be claimed as exact historical reconstructions.
- If retained for modern A–Z coverage, they must be labeled as editorial interpolations rather than 1782 source poses.
