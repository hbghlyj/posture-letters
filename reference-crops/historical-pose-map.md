# Historical 24-pose construction map

Source: *The Comical Hotch-Potch, or the Alphabet turn’d Posture-Master* (1782). The print has 24 cells; J and U are absent.

This map records the visible construction of each figure. Historical fidelity takes priority over modern anatomical plausibility.

| Glyph | Construction visible in the print |
|---|---|
| A | Two-person letter (Mitelli). Two figures lean toward one another; each raises the inner arm straight up so the flat hands press together at a sharp apex, and each lower arm bends slightly at the elbow to jointly hold a round object at hip level, which reads as the crossbar. Each stands on a straight weight-bearing outer leg with the inner leg slightly forward and gently bent. |
| B | The shared P/R stance carrying two loops. The head, upright torso and perfectly straight standing leg form the vertical stem at the left. The near arm loops outward from the shoulder in a smooth rubbery curve and tucks back inward with the hand resting at the waist, closing the upper loop. The other leg lifts, bending outward at the knee in a curved arc, and tucks back so the foot rests against the standing ankle, closing the lower loop. The far arm hangs straight down along the torso, blending into the stem. |
| C | Kneeling backward arch. Shins and feet run right along the baseline; thighs/hips and spine sweep up the left curve. The face is inside the upper-left opening, and an arm extends over the top toward the upper-right. |
| D | Deep backbend combined with a kneeling stance. The chest, hips and thighs align straight and upright as the vertical spine, the head facing left at the sharp top-left corner. The arm extends up from the shoulder and arches dramatically backward and downward behind the torso, forming the upper curve. The lower legs and feet bend backward at the kneeling knees to meet the downward-reaching hand, and hand and feet close the loop in mid-air behind the body to complete the curved belly. |
| E | Seated upright at the left: head and torso form the stem. Both crossbars are arms and both run dead horizontal, as on F — one extending right at the top and the second right at mid-height, each ending in an open hand. The broad lower leg lies horizontally along the baseline with the slimmer near leg separately visible just above it, both profile shoes turning upward. |
| F | Standing upright, both arms making the crossbars. The head, upright torso and two tightly parallel legs align straight down as the vertical trunk. The near arm extends horizontally straight out from the shoulder line as the longer upper prong; the far arm crosses over the front of the chest and extends straight out parallel to it, positioned lower on the torso, as the slightly shorter middle prong. Both feet face forward at the base. |
| G | Deeper version of C. The body sweeps around the left; the face looks into the counter. Arms meet toward the upper-right, and paired lower legs/feet run right at the bottom, producing the inward G terminals. |
| H | Two-person pose. Two figures stand straight in profile facing one another and act as the vertical sides. Each upper arm hangs vertically from the shoulder, carried proud of its own pillar so it stays a visible separate limb, and is the same length as the forearm; only the forearms turn out horizontally at elbow height and clasp at the centre, so the crossbar is made of forearms alone and sits level with the elbows. |
| I | Neutral upright person: head above a rigid straight torso, arms hanging at the sides, and two close straight legs below. The ordinary human head/trunk/leg balance is essential to this deliberately simple posture. |
| J | Kneeling side profile facing right. The head, neck and upright torso align vertically as the straight main stem. The knees turn forward and bend sharply so the lower legs — shins, ankles and feet — extend diagonally upward and backward behind the body, creating the upward-turning hook at the base. The arms hang straight down along the sides of the torso, blending into the vertical line of the stem. |
| K | Kneeling figure. The upright torso, neck, head and kneeling near leg align straight down as the vertical stem, the knee planted directly under the torso as a plumb line; the thigh drops vertically to a kneecap resting on the ground and the shin folds back at a right angle to lie flat along the floor, the foot trailing behind. The far leg extends far out to the side at a bent knee, giving the lower diagonal the extra length needed to balance the short lower stem and creating a wide, elongated base. The near arm is raised straight up and outward at about forty-five degrees as the upper diagonal, with an open hand serif. The far arm rests flat against the hip and thigh, keeping the centre of the letter solid with no open negative space between the two diagonals. |
| L | Kneeling side profile. The head, straight neck and vertical torso form the tall pillar. At the base of the torso the posture hinges sharply: the knees turn directly forward and bend completely, dropping the thighs vertically and pivoting the lower body into the horizontal plane, so the shins and ankles stretch out along the floor as the bottom bar. The feet act as a serif at the end of the stroke, heels and toes giving a slight vertical terminal. |
| M | Seated pose using the body's natural hinges. The arms drop straight down behind the back, shoulders locked, forming the left vertical stroke and supporting the torso's weight. The torso leans backward from the hips for the first internal slope. The thighs pull up toward the chest and the knees flex deeply to create the sharp central apex, and the lower legs descend as the right stroke. The left hand bends horizontally at the wrist and presses flat on the ground as the left base serif; the right foot extends forward from the ankle and plants flat as the right base serif. |
| N | Dynamic back-bending stance. The figure's straight arms are planted firmly on the ground and stand vertically, forming the left stroke and supporting the upper body. The torso and thighs slope naturally downward from the chest toward the floor, making the diagonal. The knees bend sharply and rest on the ground so both lower legs and feet extend straight upward into the air, forming the right stroke, the shin kept shorter than the whole arm. The supporting hands lie flat along the floor with the fingers running out horizontally, forming a serif at the base of the left stroke. |
| O | Deep backbend from standing, the body closing into a ring. The continuous arch of the spine and torso forms a smooth rounded crest at the peak. The buttocks and the backward-sloping thighs and knees curve down the left side and tuck inward at the ankles. The upper back, neck and arms form the right side curve, the hair falling loosely along the outer perimeter. At the base the hands are planted firmly on the ground at the right and the shod feet on the left, curving toward each other across a narrow gap to nearly close the circle. |
| P | Figure standing upright. The head, straight torso and closely planted legs align vertically to form the solid left stem. The upper loop is formed entirely by one arm, which extends outward from the shoulder and exaggerates into a smooth rubbery curve looping downward; the hand rests firmly on the hip, closing the bowl at the torso's midline. The other arm hangs straight down loosely along the opposite side, tucked away so it does not disrupt the silhouette. |
| Q | Inverted lower body. The head/shoulders lie at the bottom; paired short-breeched thighs rise around the ring, bend at explicit side knees, and taper through stockinged calves to small inward shoes joined at the apex. One shoulder-attached arm and hand projects below the lower-right as the Q tail. |
| R | The P stance with the lower limbs changed: the head, upright torso and firmly planted straight leg form the vertical left backbone, one arm arches outward from the shoulder in a smooth elongated curve and hooks back inward with the hand flat against the waist to close the loop at the body's midline, and the other leg extends sharply downward and outward to the right, planting at an angle to create R's stabilising diagonal. |
| S | Lying pose in which the longer lower limbs sweep the biggest open curves. The legs and lower torso extend up and then arch forward, forming the elongated upper hook and top terminal with the feet pointing up and to the right. Sharp flexion at the hips angles the thighs diagonally down and back to carry the centre line across the middle. The spine, upper torso and head curl along the floor to the left, the head resting at the very bottom facing upward as the lower baseline hook. |
| T | Upright figure. The straight spine, narrow hips and tightly closed legs align vertically down the centre as the pillar. Both arms extend straight out to the sides at a perfect ninety degrees from the shoulders, forming one continuous horizontal top line. At the ends of the bar the wrists flex downward so the hands point down, mimicking the hanging terminals of a classic typographic T, and the feet flare slightly outward at the base as foot serifs. |
| V | Upside-down figure. The head is at the bottom apex, its face kept upright and the gaze lifted as in Q so the figure looks up out of the apex. Two straight legs spread upward to the outer top corners; the arms lie along/grip the legs near the lower V. |
| W | Upright centered head. The torso splits into the two inner descending strokes; arms reach all the way to two shared-baseline inner hand/contact points, while two long legs rise vertically at the outer sides, creating the four-stroke W. |
| X | Spread-eagle Vitruvian diagonal. Head and short torso occupy the center; both arms rise diagonally to the upper corners and both legs spread diagonally to the lower corners. Arms and legs share one limb width, clearly under the torso, and the raised hands are open with splayed fingers. |
| Y | Seated figure contrasted with the standing T. Both arms extend straight up and outward from the shoulders in a wide V, the open palms facing upward, rather than T's flat horizontal crossbar. The lower half is condensed into a tight frontal sitting posture: the figure squats facing forward with both knees drawn close together against the chest, and the shins and feet align vertically down the front of the body to form a shorter, thicker central column. The head is nestled centrally between the shoulders at the convergence point of the upper branches and the seated torso. |
| Z | Side profile combining a dramatic backward lean with flat arm extensions. Both arms extend horizontally straight out from the shoulders as the top bar, the hands kept completely flat and parallel to the forearms so the bar tapers thinner toward the tip like a stylised pen stroke. The torso tilts backward from the knees in one precise straight diagonal, without bending at the waist, linking the upper arms to the lower base. A deep kneeling stance lays the shins and ankles flat along the floor as the bottom bar, and at its back end the heels and upturned toes lift slightly to form a vertical foot serif anchoring the bottom corner. |

## Photographic body references

Several glyphs were additionally checked against modern photographs of the same
posture, used only to correct proportion and joint anatomy — never to override
the print's pose topology:

- A spread-eagle silhouette informed **X** and **Y**: one shared limb width for
  arms and legs, both under torso width, and open hands at the raised terminals.
- A handshake photograph of two people standing in profile informed **H**:
  standing torso-to-leg balance, vertical upper arms, and a forearm-only
  crossbar at elbow height.
- A standing frontal figure informed **T**, and the same balance was then
  applied to the other upright standing poses **F** and **P**: hips near the
  middle of the stem with legs matching the trunk in length.

## Current font-outline refinement note

All visible legs now share the print’s period costume anatomy without sharing generic geometry: full short trousers gather into a clear cuff just below each source-positioned knee, stockinged lower legs widen into a noticeable calf muscle, an inset engraved contour reinforces the gastrocnemius/shin transition, and ankles taper into the historically directed shoes. The system follows each pose through standing, seated, kneeling, horizontal, and inverted orientations rather than straightening the body into standardized strokes. M retains custom exaggerated calf pillars with explicit knee cuffs; N’s blue diagonal reaches a grounded bent knee before turning into the raised gray calf; and Q’s circular lower-body construction now consists of two cuffed, bent legs with tapered calves and joined apex shoes. O alone preserves the source’s hidden aerial lower torso with only compressed feet visible.

The current font follows A’s continuous forward fold while reversing both shoes left and differentiating wide breeches, knee cuffs, calves, and shin cuts. B now separates the planted leg from the outward-and-inward bent right leg, keeps a visible knee, and shortens both loop arms and the lower-bowl leg to near-normal anatomy. D and H mirror only their asymmetric facial and hat details, preserving their established body topology. E extends its vertical torso directly into the baseline leg. I keeps its rigid source stance while restoring realistic head, trunk, arm, thigh, and calf ratios. K drops its upper elbow before a rising forearm whose compact palm projects only the thumb and index as sharp serifs, with the other three fingers folded inward; full short breeches and engraved muscle contours remain on both lower legs. M keeps its face, cupping hands, paired buttocks, central cleft, cuffed muscular pillars, and removes the former hanging center appendage. N puts head and knee on one ground plane, preserves the raised inner corner, and outlines a human-length foreground sleeve across the blue diagonal with a projecting hand. O hides the lower torso, inverts its facial hierarchy, lifts its pupils toward the counter, smooths the shoulders and elbows into the ring, marks visible side knee/elbow joints, and gives the apex a visible interlocking hand seam. P shortens both separately tapered backward arm routes while retaining elbow, sleeve, palm, and clasp details above its cuffed legs and outward shoes. Q rebuilds its ring as articulated inverted legs, inverts its bottom face like O, keeps the tail arm at a normal anatomical length, and gives that tail a visible hand. R turns its planted shoe clearly to the source-right orientation. W lowers the outer knees to the baseline and draws distinct supporting index fingers and thumbs on the inner hands. X shortens its raised arms to a normal diagonal reach while keeping its long spread legs, and Y shortens both the raised arms and torso while lengthening the close paired leg stem. G/O/Q remain free of the former dangling decorative artifacts; O’s apex seam and Q’s cuff/calf cuts are functional anatomy. H also keeps separate upper-arm and thigh roots, with open side gaps for contour clarity.

Across A–Z, hats and shoes now act as anatomical serif terminals: cocked-hat brims project from source-visible hats, and shoes follow the toe directions visible in the source at the actual feet. They remain attached to their body endpoints instead of moving to conventional type-stroke corners. F, K, and R point both profile shoes right; E, G, and L turn their shoes upward; P and X use outward left/right pairs. O uses compressed outward shoes for its aerial foreshortening, while Q’s compact pair points inward at the ring apex. N’s left shoe stays on the inverted body endpoint while its high right shoe terminates the raised calf. M keeps its small hatless upward face in the central dip and uses outward shoes on its outer calf pillars rather than inventing a displaced hat. These are standard scalable outlines with punched contour details, not separate raster artwork.

## Editorial letters

- **J** and **U** do not occur in the print and therefore cannot be claimed as exact historical reconstructions.
- If retained for modern A–Z coverage, they must be labeled as editorial interpolations rather than 1782 source poses.
